How limiters apply

A request is blocked as soon as any limiter that applies to it is over budget:

  • Global limiters (blank scope) apply to every session; session-specific limiters only apply to the one session they identify. Both kinds can be in force for the same request at once — any one of them being over budget is enough to block.

Two independent switches decide whether a limiter is currently enforced — both must hold for it to count:

  • The Status toggle is a manual on/off switch that stays exactly where you leave it.
  • The active period is a recurring hour-of-day window (server time, wraps past midnight when "From" is later than "To") — outside it, the limiter neither blocks requests nor accrues spend, even while switched on. "Always active" covers all 24 hours.

Refresh resets a limiter's spend back to zero and opens a new tracking window:

  • By default it's a fixed interval counted from whenever the limiter last refreshed (or was created) — e.g. every 60 minutes on the dot from that moment, regardless of the wall clock.
  • Aligned instead snaps to the next calendar boundary — top of the hour, midnight, Monday, or the 1st of the month — so every reset lands in sync with the clock rather than with creation time. Only offered for the handful of durations with an unambiguous boundary (60 minutes, 1 or 24 hours, 1 or 7 days, 1 month).

Editing limiters

Saving an edit always resets current spend to $0 and restarts the refresh window from that moment.

The Status toggle is the one exception: switching a limiter on or off is a separate action that never touches its spend or refresh window.
